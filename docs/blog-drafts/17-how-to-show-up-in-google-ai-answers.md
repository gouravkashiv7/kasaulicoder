# How to Show Up in Google's AI Answers: What Google Itself Says

You search for something on Google and, before the usual list of links, you get a written answer. That is an **AI Overview**. There is also **AI Mode**, where you can keep asking follow-up questions, like a chat.

If you run a business, the natural question is: how does my website get mentioned there?

There is a lot of advice online, and much of it is sold by people with a tool to sell. So we went to the source. Google has published its own guide for website owners. This post explains what it says in plain words. We link to it at the end so you can check us.

## First, is this worth caring about?

Google says AI Overviews has over 2.5 billion monthly users and AI Mode has over one billion. Those are Google's own numbers about its own product, so read them that way. But the direction is clear: many people now read an AI answer before they click anything.

The good news is that these answers are built from ordinary web pages. Google's guide says its AI features are rooted in the same ranking systems as normal Search. The AI looks up pages in Google's index, reads them, and links to the ones it used.

So you are not starting again. A page that Google cannot find or does not trust will not appear in an AI answer either.

## What Google asks you to do

### 1. Say something only you can say

This is the point Google stresses most. It separates two kinds of content:

- **Commodity content:** general advice anyone could write, such as a list of common tips.
- **Non-commodity content:** something that comes from your own experience, such as how you solved a real problem and what it cost.

An AI can already write the first kind. It needs the second kind from you.

For a small business, this is an advantage. You know things a big website does not: your town, your customers, your prices, the mistakes you have seen. Write those down.

### 2. Make the page easy to read

Use clear headings, short paragraphs and plain words. Google says to write for people, and to organise the page so a reader can find their way around. Good photos and short videos help too, because AI answers can show them.

### 3. Keep the technical basics in order

The guide lists nothing exotic here:

- Google must be able to **crawl** your pages (visit and read them) and **index** them (store them in its list).
- The site should work well on a phone and load quickly.
- Avoid having the same content on many different addresses.
- If your site depends heavily on JavaScript, it needs extra care so Google can read it.

If you have never checked these, start with Google Search Console. It is free and tells you which pages Google has indexed.

### 4. Keep your business details up to date

AI answers can include local businesses and products. Google points to two free tools: **Google Business Profile** for your address, hours and services, and **Merchant Center** if you sell products. Wrong opening hours in an AI answer are worse than no answer, so check them.

## What Google says you can ignore

This part of the guide is the most useful, because it can save you money. For Google Search, you do not need:

- **An llms.txt file** or any other special "AI file". Google says it ignores them. We wrote a separate post on this: [Do you need an llms.txt file?](/blogs/do-you-need-llms-txt)
- **"Chunking"**, which means cutting your content into tiny pieces for the AI. Google says there is no ideal page length.
- **Rewriting your pages in a special style for AI.** It understands ordinary language and synonyms.
- **Fake mentions** of your brand scattered around the web.
- **Special structured data for AI.** Structured data is still useful for normal search results, but there is no extra AI version.

Google also warns against making a separate page for every possible way of asking a question. Done to game the results, that breaks its spam rules.

## How to check where you stand

1. **Search for what you sell.** Ask the question your customer would ask. See which sites the AI answer links to, and read them. What do they say that your site does not?
2. **Open Search Console.** Confirm your important pages are indexed. Google has announced a report that shows how your pages appear in its AI features, and a setting to opt in or out. It said these would reach site owners in stages, so you may or may not see them yet.
3. **Read your own page as a stranger.** Does it answer the question in the first few lines? Would you trust it?
4. **Check your Business Profile.** Hours, phone, services, photos.

## What nobody can promise you

Google's guide says plainly that meeting every requirement does not guarantee your page will be crawled, indexed or shown. It also says AI Overviews do not appear for every search.

So be careful with anyone who guarantees a place in AI answers. We do not promise positions in search, and we will not promise them in AI answers either. What can be done is the work above: a clear, fast, honest site that says something real.

## The short version

- AI answers are built from normal web pages, using Google's normal ranking systems.
- Write from your own experience. General tips are the first thing AI replaces.
- Keep the site crawlable, fast and tidy, and keep your business details correct.
- Skip the special files and tricks. Google says it ignores them.
- Nobody can guarantee a place in an AI answer.
