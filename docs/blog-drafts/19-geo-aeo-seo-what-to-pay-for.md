# GEO, AEO and SEO: Do You Need to Pay for "AI Optimization"?

You have a website. Perhaps you already pay someone for SEO. Now an email arrives offering a "GEO package" so that AI tools will recommend your business.

Is this a new thing you need to buy? Or the same thing with a new name?

Here is what the words mean, what Google says, and seven questions to ask before you pay.

## The three acronyms

- **SEO** is *search engine optimization*: the work of making your website easy for search engines to find, understand and trust.
- **AEO** is *answer engine optimization*: the same goal, aimed at tools that give a direct answer instead of a list of links.
- **GEO** is *generative engine optimization*: again the same goal, aimed at AI tools that write an answer, such as Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT or Perplexity.

The names are different. The question is whether the work is.

## What Google says

Google has published a guide for website owners about its AI features. On this point it is direct. From Google Search's point of view, improving how you appear in AI answers is still SEO.

The reason is how the AI answers are made. Google's AI looks up pages using the same ranking systems as normal Search, reads them, and links to what it used. A page that does well in one has done the groundwork for the other.

The guide goes further and lists popular "AI tricks" that you can ignore for Google Search:

- special AI files such as llms.txt
- cutting pages into tiny "chunks"
- rewriting your content in a special style for AI
- fake mentions of your brand around the web
- special structured data made for AI

It also warns about tools that promise ranking success or claim to use "internal" Google numbers. Google says no outside tool has access to those.

## A fair limit on that answer

Google speaks for Google. Other AI tools have their own ways of choosing sources, and they have published much less about how they work.

So we cannot tell you exactly how every AI tool picks what to mention, and neither can anyone else. What we can say is that the basics are the same everywhere: an AI tool can only mention a page it can reach, read and has some reason to trust.

## What is genuinely new

It would be wrong to say nothing has changed. A few things have:

- **People ask longer questions.** Someone may type a whole situation instead of three keywords. Pages that answer real, specific questions do better.
- **General tips are worth less.** An AI can write a generic list by itself. What it cannot invent is your experience: your cases, your prices, your local knowledge.
- **There is something new to measure.** Google has announced reporting in Search Console on how pages appear in its AI features. It is arriving in stages, so you may not see it yet.
- **Your business details matter more.** AI answers can show local businesses, so wrong hours or an old phone number travel further.

None of this needs a new kind of agency. It needs the old work done properly.

## Red flags in an "AI optimization" offer

- **A guaranteed place in AI answers.** Google itself says showing your page is never guaranteed.
- **A secret method.** Good SEO is not secret. It is published in Google's own documents.
- **A price built on files and tricks** from the list above.
- **Hundreds of AI-written pages** to "cover every question". Google treats this as spam when it is done to game results.
- **A score from a tool, with no explanation** of what will change on your website.
- **Fear.** "SEO is dead, act now."

## Seven questions to ask before you pay

1. What exactly will you change on my website?
2. Which of those changes come from Google's published guidance?
3. How will we measure the result, and where will I see it?
4. What can you not promise?
5. Who writes the content, and will it be based on my business or on general advice?
6. What do I keep if I stop paying?
7. Can you show me the monthly report another client receives, with the name removed?

A good provider will be comfortable with all seven.

## So, should you pay for it?

Pay for good SEO, if your website matters to your business. That means a site search engines can read, pages that load quickly, content from your own experience, correct business details, and an honest report of what was done.

Do not pay a second time for the same work under a new name.

That is how we work. We do not sell a separate GEO package. The work that helps you in AI answers is part of our normal SEO work, and we do not promise positions in either.

## The short version

- GEO and AEO describe the same aim as SEO, pointed at AI answers.
- Google says that for its own search, this is still SEO.
- Other AI tools publish less, so be wary of anyone who claims to know their secrets.
- What has changed: longer questions, less value in generic content, new reporting.
- Ask what will change on your site, how it will be measured and what cannot be promised.
